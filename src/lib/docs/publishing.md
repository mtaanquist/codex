# Publishing your work

Publishing turns a finished story into a public reading page that anyone with the link can read. It never exposes your drafts: publishing takes a frozen snapshot, and the reading pages only ever show that snapshot. You keep writing; the public edition stays as it was until you publish again.

## Before you can publish

An administrator enables publishing for your account. Once it is on, claim a handle on your account page, under Profile. Your handle is the address your public pages live at, and it is permanent, so choose it with care.

## Publishing a story

Open the story's settings and publish it. You can give the edition a label, such as "First edition". Publishing again replaces the public version with a fresh snapshot of your current text.

If the story has [saved drafts](/docs/drafts), "What to publish" lets you publish one of them instead of the working draft.

## Visibility

Each story has a visibility:

- **Private** keeps it to yourself.
- **Unlisted** is reachable by direct link but is not listed on your public page or on a universe page.
- **Public** is listed on your page at your handle.

## Listing a story in Browse

If the site has a Browse page (a link to it shows in the bar of the public pages), readers can find stories from every writer on the site there. A public story only shows on Browse if you choose to list it: in the Publish section of the story's settings, tick "List in Browse" and save. Untick it to take the story off Browse at once. Setting the story to private or unlisted takes it off too.

To keep all your stories and your name off Browse, turn on "Stay off Browse" on your account page. Your own page and your stories stay reachable as before.

A moderator can remove a story from Browse. When they do, the Publish section of the story's settings says so and gives the reason, and you cannot list it again until they allow it. The story's own pages stay up.

## Finding stories on Browse

On the Browse page, search by title, writer, description or world, or pick a genre or fandom. Under "Hide stories with", tick any content warning you want to avoid. Adult stories are left out unless you tick "Include adult content"; your choice is remembered on this device. On a story's reading page, a genre, fandom or tag links to Browse with that filter.

## Adult content

Tick "Adult content" in the Publish section of a story's settings if the story is for adults. Readers who open its reading page first see a notice and choose to go on, and search engines are asked to leave the page out. The label is saved with each edition, so after you tick or untick it on a story that is already published, publish again for readers to see the change.

## Genres, content warnings, tags and fandoms

In the Publish section of a story's settings, under "Save tags", you can describe the story for readers:

- **Genres.** Pick up to three from the site's list.
- **Content warnings.** Tick everything on the site's list that a reader should know about before they start, such as graphic violence or death. If you tick none, the reading page says you added none; it does not call the story safe.
- **Fandoms.** If the story is set in an existing world, such as a game setting or a series, pick it from the site's list. You can pick up to five. If the world you want is missing, use a tag instead, or ask the people who run the site to add it.
- **Tags.** Anything else that helps a reader find the story, such as "found family" or "slow burn". Type a tag and press Enter or type a comma. When the site has a Browse page, the box suggests tags already used on stories listed there. You can add up to 20, each up to 50 characters. Tags that differ only in capitals, spaces, hyphens or underscores count as the same tag.

Genres, fandoms and tags show on the reading page as soon as you save. Content warnings belong to the edition, like the adult flag, so readers see a change after you publish again. The moderators can merge tags that mean the same thing, or remove a tag; a removed tag disappears from your story and cannot be added again.

## Edition downloads

When you publish, Codex also prepares download files of the edition: a markdown archive, an EPUB, and a PDF. Find them in the story's settings under "Edition downloads". They capture the edition exactly as published, so they do not change when you keep writing. The PDF shows only images you uploaded to Codex; an image linked from another website is left out.

If you want readers to download your story too, turn on "Let readers download this edition" in the same place. Readers then see EPUB and PDF links on the story's public page. The markdown archive is yours alone; it is never offered to readers.

The files are prepared shortly after publishing. If they have not appeared, use "Generate again".

## Page setup

Page setup controls how print and PDF output is typeset: page size (including book trim sizes), margins, font and size, paragraph style, line spacing, the binding gutter, the scene-break text, and page numbers or a running title on the PDF. Set your defaults on your account page under Display; a story can override any of them in its own settings.

Line spacing (single through double) and paragraph style also show in the preview, so what you read while writing matches the export. The binding gutter adds extra margin on the inner, spine edge, alternating between left and right pages so a bound book has room at the seam; because that alternates per page, it shows in the Print view rather than the continuous preview, and it only affects PDF and print.

Changing the page setup does not change a published edition's text. Use "Generate again" under Edition downloads to re-typeset the files with your current setup. The EPUB stays reflowable - reading apps control its page size and margins - but the paragraph style, line spacing, and scene-break text carry over.

To force a page break inside a chapter, put \page alone on its own line in your prose. It breaks the page in print and PDF and is invisible everywhere else.

## Your public page

Your handle page is your shelf: your name, your handle, and every story you have set to public, each with its title, when it was published, and its description. When your profile is set to public it also shows a short bio, any links you add, and whether you are open for commissions. You control all of that on your account page, under Profile.

A story you publish gets its own reading page under your handle, at your handle followed by the story's address: for example /@yourhandle/the-long-road. The address is the one in your story's own link in Codex, so renaming the story changes it. Once a story has been published, links to its old address keep working and take readers to the new one, and none of your other stories can take the old address. Deleting a story is different: moving it to the trash frees its address at once, so its page stops answering there and a story you create or rename later can take that address. If you restore the story, it gets its old address back, unless another story took it in the meantime; then it comes back at a new address made from its title, and links to the old address no longer reach it. Older links that use the story's id instead keep working too. An unlisted story's link always uses its id, a long string of letters and numbers, so nobody can find it by guessing its title. Once the story is published, not private and open to readers, the Publish section of its settings shows the link readers use, ready to copy. The page opens with the title, who wrote it, how long it is, and the story's description, then the contents and the prose itself.

On your public pages (the reading page, your bio and your shelf), only images you uploaded to Codex show. An image linked from another website shows as a link with the image's description as its text, and a reader opens it only if they choose to. In your shelf and on Browse, a story's description shows as plain text, without links, images or formatting.

When at least one story in a universe is published and set to public, the universe gets a page too, at /@yourhandle/universes/ followed by the universe's address. It shows the universe's name and the stories in it that are listed on your shelf. The reading page of every published story in the universe, unlisted ones included, links to it just above the title. It shows nothing else from the universe. A one-off story that is not in a universe has no universe page. Renaming a universe also changes its address, and its old address is always kept for it, whether or not anything in it has been published yet: links to the old address take readers to the new one, and none of your other universes can take it. Deleting a universe also works like deleting a story: moving it to the trash frees its address, so a universe you create or rename later can take it. A restored universe gets its old address back unless another universe took it in the meantime, in which case it comes back at a new address made from its name.

## What a reader sees

The reading pages are the outward face of your work, so they are plain and quiet: one column of prose, chapter headings, and nothing between the reader and the words. Until you upload cover art, a cover is the story's own title set in the reading typeface, on the colour you chose under "Cover colour" in the story's settings.

Both pages name you the way you asked to be named: your pen name if you set one on your account page, otherwise your display name. The first time you claim a handle or publish with no pen name set, a dialog asks which name should go on the page, offering your display name to keep; what you choose becomes your pen name, and you can change it on your account page. Your handle stays the address in the link.

Under the title, the reading page lists the story's genres, fandoms, tags and content warnings. When the site has a Browse page, each genre, fandom and tag links to the other stories there that share it.

A reader gets two controls in the bar: the theme, which starts in whichever of light or dark their device asks for and remembers a choice they make from its menu (Light, Warm, Dark, or System), and the question mark, which opens these pages. At the end of both pages a footer says the work was written in Codex, who published it, and offers a link for a reader who wants to write something of their own. A story's reading page also has a "Report this story" link.

## Taking something down

Set a story back to private, or republish after edits. An administrator or moderator can also take an edition down if needed. When they do, the Publish section of the story's settings says so and gives the reason they chose. You cannot publish the story again until they restore it.

## Reporting a story

If a story breaks the rules of the site, copies someone else's work, or is missing content warnings or the adult flag, use "Report this story" at the bottom of its reading page. Pick a reason, add a short comment if it helps, and send it. You do not need an account, and the report does not include your name or email address. The people who look after the site read every report and can take the story down or remove it from Browse.

While an administrator has your account suspended, your public page, your stories' reading pages and universe pages, and their downloads are hidden from readers. They come back as they were when the suspension is lifted.
